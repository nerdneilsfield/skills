# Dialogue craft and fidelity

## Characters

Give each host a job, a way of speaking, and a stake. Write these in the script header and keep them fixed.

| Role | Job | Voice traits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Guide | knows the material, owns facts | calm, concrete, admits uncertainty |
| Stand-in | asks what a smart outsider would ask, restates | short sentences, plain words, says "I lost you at ..." |
| Skeptic | tests evidence, asks "compared to what" | dry, precise, disagrees with a reason |

Two hosts: Guide and Stand-in, and the Guide must sometimes be wrong or incomplete and corrected. Three: add the Skeptic, who must hold a position, not only play devil's advocate. Differences should show in sentence length, favourite examples, and what each notices, not in tag labels.

## Moves that make it sound like conversation

- **The stand-in asks the listener's question at the moment of confusion**, not on a schedule. If a term arrived without an example, the stand-in says so.
- **Interruption.** A host may cut in mid-thought to ask or object. In text, end the interrupted line with a dash and no full stop, and start the next with the interjection. Use sparingly; every interruption must carry content. TTS cannot overlap voices, so interruptions are sequential: write them as short lines with a short pause (0.1 to 0.2 s).
- **Real disagreement.** The two hold different readings of the same evidence, and each gives a reason that comes from the material. It ends with a position, a concession, or an honest "the paper does not settle this". Do not stage a disagreement the material does not support.
- **Callbacks.** Return to an early example later ("remember the three-word example? here it breaks"). It rewards listeners and cuts new explanation.
- **Restating.** The stand-in restates a hard idea; the guide corrects the restatement if needed. This is the main teaching device.
- **Silence and shortness.** Mix a 100-character turn with 6-character turns ("所以呢?", "不一定。"). Not every line needs an answer.

## What to avoid

- Filler banter and fake enthusiasm: "太棒了", "哇", "great question", "真的很有意思", "你说得太对了". Replace with the specific reaction: what surprised the host and why.
- Praise as transition. Move on with content: "那有个问题: ...".
- Hosts reciting the outline ("接下来我们聊三点"). Show the shape by doing it.
- Every line ending in a question; every answer starting with "对".
- Both hosts speaking in the same register and length.
- Quoting the material at length. Paraphrase; quote only short, verbatim, attributed lines.
- Running gags that do not go anywhere; self-referential podcast talk.

## Fidelity

1. Facts come from the material or from sources listed in the ledger. If a host adds background from general knowledge, it goes in the ledger as background with a source, or is cut.
2. Mark confidence in speech: "the paper reports ...", "the authors argue ...", "I can't verify this, it's from a press report". Unverified or conflicting items are said to be uncertain, with who says what.
3. Numbers are copied, not recalled. Check each against the ledger in the revision pass.
4. **Fictional insiders.** A composite expert or caller is permitted when it helps the format (for example the news interview). It must be marked `fictional` in the script header and disclosed aloud in the first minute; it must not state facts absent from the ledger; it must not be modelled on a real, identifiable person; it never claims credentials as fact beyond the disclosure ("an imagined engineer who works on this kind of system").
5. **Real people.** Never put invented words, opinions, or private details in the mouth of a real person, or imitate a real person's voice without consent. Describing what a real person said is fine if the material says it, with attribution.
6. The hosts are AI voices; do not have them claim personal experience ("when I was at ...", "I tried this last week"). Use "people who have built this report ..." or "in the paper's setup ...".
7. Leave out what the material cannot support rather than filling the time. A shorter true episode beats a longer padded one.

## Revision pass for dialogue

Read each exchange and ask: could a listener tell who is speaking with the names removed? Does every question get a real answer? Is there at least one place where a host is convinced or not convinced? Does any line exist only to sound friendly? Delete it.
